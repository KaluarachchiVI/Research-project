Background

Why static timers fail real study sessions

Digital study environments depend on self-regulation. Most consumer timers use static cycles and cannot adapt to real-time variation in fatigue, task complexity, or impulsive disengagement. The Adaptive Cognitive-Load Study Timer for Personalised Break Scheduling (25-26J-458) addresses this through four integrated subsystems: a Cognitive Load Estimator, an Adaptive Break Scheduler, a chronotype-aware time-of-day recommender, and an Intent-Lock overlay.

One-size-fits-all approaches lead to suboptimal breaks, flow interruption, and weak enforcement of helpful pauses. Learners also lack an integrated, privacy-preserving system that adapts to load, circadian style, and exit intent in one stack.

Research impact — targets
  • Stronger self-regulation through contextual work/break decisions.
  • Reduced sustained overload via dual-signal CLE + micro-EMA gating.
  • Time-of-day and chronotype-aware study windows with interpretable visualisation.

Literature

What the field knows — and where the gap is

Cognitive load and fatigue, passive sensing, micro-EMA, bandits (LinUCB / TS), chronotype, and graduated self-control. At integration level, gaps include static consumer timers, weak calibration, under-use of dayparts, and evaluation on regret alone.

This project closes those gaps with dual-signal CLE+EMA, a 16-arm scheduler, mabwiser for time-of-day, multi-metric evaluation, and Intent-Lock friction.

Current limitations
  • ✗ Static intervals without per-session cognitive context
  • ✗ Sensing without calibration or privacy-first boundaries
  • ✗ Chronotype and daypart under-used in many tools
  • ✗ All-or-nothing blocking instead of graduated friction
Our contribution
  • ✓ 14-D passive CLE, Kalman + RLS, micro-EMA, SSE to UI
  • ✓ 16-arm LinUCB / Thompson and eight-metric harness
  • ✓ mabwiser dayparts, optional GBR weekly hybrid, ribbons
  • ✓ Logistic exit scoring with three-level friction (Intent-Lock)
Objectives

What we set out to deliver

Main objective Build an integrated study timer that adapts breaks to estimated cognitive load, dayparts, and exit intent — with transparent privacy controls and evaluation across multiple service metrics.
Specific objectives
SO1

Dual-signal cognitive-load estimator

14-D features, normalisation, Kalman + RLS fusion, micro-EMA gating.

SO2

16-arm break scheduler

LinUCB & Thompson over a work/break grid with reward shaping and an eight-metric eval harness.

SO3

Chronotype recommender

mabwiser dayparts, optional GBR weekly hybrid, intensity-ribbon UI.

SO4

Intent-Lock friction

LogisticRegression + three-level graduated friction; hybrid edge–cloud deploy.
